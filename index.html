Victorian local government • A data story

Local services. Different experiences.

How does the delivery and use of essential council services vary across Victorian communities? Explore waste, libraries, aquatic recreation and residents' views, using reported council indicators.

Same responsibilities, different communities

Waste collection, libraries, aquatic facilities and community consultation touch everyday life, but council geographies, populations and service models vary. This story asks where differences occur and how those patterns change—without assuming any single indicator captures overall service quality.

Scroll through the sections, hover for council values, and use the final comparison to highlight a council.

Read the dates carefully

The performance indicators use 2024–25 unless a chart notes otherwise. The garbage-collection dataset is for 2023–24. Values are reported measures, not causal explanations.

What the evidence suggests

Three things worth taking away

Geography matters

Councils differ substantially in their waste diversion and service measures. Explore individual areas before treating averages as representative.

Trends are uneven

Waste diversion changed in different directions between 2020–21 and 2024–25. Changes in indicators should not be confused with explanations for those changes.

One number isn't a verdict

Library membership, aquatic visits, spending and satisfaction describe different dimensions of local government and should not be combined into a universal quality score.

Data, methods and limitations

Sources: Victorian Local Government Performance Reporting Framework (2020–21 through 2024–25); Victorian Local Government Annual Survey waste data (2023–24); Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2025 Local Government Area boundaries (GDA2020). Full source descriptions appear alongside each visualisation.

Method: Council results are displayed as reported or converted into labelled percentages or per-resident ratios. Map boundaries are geographic; Chart 2 uses non-overlapping graduated circles with approximate displaced locations; the Dorling cartogram shifts circles to limit overlap. The parallel-coordinates plot rescales each indicator independently. Some indicators exclude councils without applicable records.

Interpretation: These measures describe reported outcomes, quantities, service usage and costs, not the causes of difference or an overall evaluation of council effectiveness. The 2023–24 and 2024–25 results are not presented as matching-year observations.
